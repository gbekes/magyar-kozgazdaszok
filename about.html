About — methodology and scope

A technical appendix: who is in the catalogue, who is not, how the summaries are made, and editorial info. For everyday use, start at the homepage.

Who is included

Economists are admitted to the catalogue if their journal record clears two thresholds: at least one publication in a top general-interest or top field journal (the qualifying paper), and at least three publications in international peer-reviewed journals on the whitelist. The whitelist covers economics, finance, and management — roughly the top 100 such journals, plus a handful of broad science outlets. See the full list on the journals page.

"Hungarian" is interpreted broadly: Hungarian nationality, born in Hungary, or three or more years at a Hungarian institution. Diaspora is explicitly included.

Once admitted, all of an author's peer-reviewed papers and recent (last 10 years) working papers are eligible for the catalogue. Borderline admissions remain editorial decisions.

What this catalogue is not

The author filter is academic: a researcher gets in only if their journal record clears the bar above. That choice has consequences worth being honest about.

  • It does not catalogue policy-institute analysts (Budapest Institute, Hétfa, TÁRKI-Tudok, Korrupciókutató Központ) whose impact runs primarily through reports and op-eds rather than peer-reviewed journal articles. Their work is often excellent and directly useful to Hungarian policymakers — the catalogue's silence on them is a scope choice, not a quality judgment.
  • It does not catalogue editors of Hungarian policy publications like Munkaerőpiaci Tükör, Közoktatás Indikátorrendszere, or Verseny és Szabályozás, even though those volumes shape how the profession reaches government.
  • It does not catalogue the data-infrastructure work that makes top-journal publications possible — the people who built the KSH-KRTK research room, who set up linked administrative panel datasets, who negotiated data-access agreements.
  • It does not catalogue policy blog and public-facing writing (De Facto, Qubit, Portfolio blog) on its own, though the Press track indexes such items when they connect to an admitted author's research.

Where some of this work does show up: the catalogue's Policy and Press tracks index individual policy reports, op-eds, podcast appearances, and book chapters — including by authors not on the main list. If you know of a relevant Munkaerőpiaci Tükör chapter, a Qubit column, or a Budapest Institute report we should be indexing, please tell us.

How the non-technical summaries are made

Each paper on the site has three short, policy-facing fields: a plain-language summary, a description of the data used, and a note on policy relevance. These are drafted with AI help from the abstract and (where available) the introduction and conclusion — and reviewed by a human editor before publication. Labels on each paper indicate review status: ai-drafted / human-reviewed / author-approved.

Limitations you should know about

  • This is a beta. Many summaries are first-draft AI output — clearly labelled. Trust the paper's DOI link more than the summary.
  • Author selection was mostly AI-driven with light editorial review. Suggestions for missing names are very welcome.
  • Some papers have Hungarian translations of the summary, some don't (yet). Missing translations fall back to English with a note.
