About — methodology and scope

A technical appendix: who is in the catalogue, who is not, how the summaries are made, and editorial info. For everyday use, start at the homepage.

Who is included

Economists are admitted to the catalogue if their journal record clears two thresholds: at least one publication in a top general-interest or top field journal (the qualifying paper), and at least three publications in international peer-reviewed journals on the whitelist. The whitelist covers economics, finance, and management — roughly the top 100 such journals, plus a handful of broad science outlets. See the full list on the journals page.

"Hungarian" is interpreted broadly, and the diaspora is obviously included.

Once admitted, all of an author's peer-reviewed papers and recent (last 10 years) working papers are eligible for the catalogue. Borderline admissions remain editorial decisions.

This catalogue has many shortcomings — one is its rigidity, focusing on academic economists with international publications. There are of course many economists very active in research published in Hungarian, as well as creating high-value, high-quality policy documents. Social scientists outside economics, finance, and management are also not included.

How the non-technical summaries are made

Each paper on the site has three short, policy-facing fields: a plain-language summary, a description of the data used, and a note on policy relevance. These are drafted with AI help from the abstract and (where available) the introduction and conclusion — and reviewed by a human editor before publication. Labels on each paper indicate review status: ai-drafted / human-reviewed / author-approved.

Limitations you should know about

  • This is a beta. Many summaries are first-draft AI output — clearly labelled. Trust the paper's DOI link more than the summary.
  • Author selection was mostly AI-driven with light editorial review. Suggestions for missing names are very welcome.
  • Some papers have Hungarian translations of the summary, some don't (yet). Missing translations fall back to English with a note.
