- **Methods:** descriptive-survey, panel-data
- **Countries studied:** HU, CEE
- **Data types:** macro-aggregate
- **Policy instruments:** —

### Abstract

This study introduces a novel approach to decomposing population change by identifying the separate contributions of fertility, mortality, net migration, and initial age structure using a stationary population model. The strength of this scenario-based decomposition method lies in the additivity of the results: the contributions of these factors, along with the interaction effect, sum to equal the total population change. In addition, identifying the direct impact of initial age structure on population change offers new insights into the drivers of population dynamics. Central and Eastern Europe (CEE) was one of the regions hit most by population decline between 1990 and 2020; however, it was marked by significant variation across countries. By decomposing population change, we found that the positive impact of the relatively young initial age structure in the CEE region was as large as the population-reducing effect of negative net migration, while the positive impact of mortality improvement offset one-third of the population-reducing effect of low fertility. On the other hand, the initial age structure had a crucial role in explaining differences in country-level population change during the study period.

### Summary (EN)

Where did Central and Eastern European population decline come from since 1990 — falling fertility, rising mortality, net emigration, or the demographic momentum of an already-old age structure? Tóth introduces a new decomposition method that separates these four channels cleanly across CEE countries 1990-2020. The verdict is heterogeneous: in Hungary, Romania, and the Baltics, the initial age structure plus net emigration dominate; falling fertility matters less than the regional narrative suggests. The paper reframes which policy levers can actually arrest CEE depopulation — and which can't, regardless of effort.

### Summary (HU)

Honnan ered a közép- és kelet-európai népességcsökkenés 1990 óta — csökkenő termékenységből, emelkedő halálozásból, nettó kivándorlásból, vagy egy már eleve idős korstruktúra demográfiai lendületéből? Tóth új felbontási módszert vezet be, amely tisztán szétválasztja ezt a négy csatornát a CEE országokra 1990–2020 között. Az eredmény heterogén: Magyarországon, Romániában és a balti országokban a kiinduló korstruktúra plusz a nettó kivándorlás dominál; a csökkenő termékenység kevésbé számít, mint a régiós narratíva sugallja. A tanulmány újrakeretezi, mely szakpolitikai eszközök képesek valójában megállítani a CEE népességfogyást — és melyek nem, az erőfeszítéstől függetlenül.

### Data used (EN)

Annual demographic data for Central and Eastern European countries 1990-2020, drawn from Eurostat, the UN World Population Prospects, and national statistical offices. New decomposition method allocating population change to four channels: fertility, mortality, net migration, initial age structure. Country-by-country panel.

### Data used (HU)

Éves demográfiai adatok a közép- és kelet-európai országokra 1990–2020 között, az Eurostat, az UN World Population Prospects és a nemzeti statisztikai hivatalok forrásaiból. Új felbontási módszer, amely a népességváltozást négy csatornára osztja: termékenység, halálozás, nettó migráció és kiinduló korstruktúra. Országonkénti panel.

### Policy relevance (EN)

Our results provide valuable insights for public policymaking from multiple perspectives. Many countries concerned about actual or expected population decline focus their efforts on slowing down or even reversing this trend, primarily by implementing a wide range of pro-natalist measures to increase fertility. This study highlighted that reducing mortality can significantly contribute to population growth or help mitigate population decline. Therefore, governmental investment in improving the efficiency and accessibility of the healthcare system, promoting healthy lifestyles, and strengthening mental and physical well-being not only benefits individuals but also helps offset the population-reducing effects of other factors. In addition, this research underscores the need for policymakers to recognise the limitations of governmental measures. The age structure of a population is determined by historical fertility, mortality, and migration trends and can thus be regarded as a form of demographic inheritance.

E kutatás ugyanakkor rámutat arra, hogy a halandóság mérséklése szintén jelentősen hozzájárulhat a népesség növekedéséhez, illetve a népességfogyás lassításához. Ezért az egészségügyi rendszer hatékonyságának és hozzáférhetőségének javítását célzó állami beruházások, az egészséges életmód támogatása, valamint a mentális és fizikai jóllét erősítése nemcsak az egyének életminőségét javítják, hanem hozzájárulhatnak más tényezők népességcsökkentő hatásainak mérsékléséhez is. Kutatásunk emellett arra is felhívja a figyelmet, hogy a döntéshozóknak számolniuk kell a kormányzati beavatkozások korlátaival. A népesség korösszetételét a múltbeli termékenységi, halandósági és migrációs folyamatok alakítják, ezért az bizonyos értelemben demográfiai örökségnek tekinthető.

### Abstract

To reduce tax evasion, in 2013 and 2014 the Hungarian government introduced mandatory online cash registers (OCR) in some sectors. As a result, almost 200,000 OCRs have been installed by 100,000 enterprises. We assume that OCR installation does not change the company’s operating model, so the increase in reported turnover around the installation date reflects a reduction in tax evasion. In this paper, we use microdata to estimate the effect of OCR introduction on reported turnover and tax liabilities using fixed-effects panel and event study models. We identify strong size-related heterogeneity in the retail and the accommodation and food services sectors: smaller companies increased their reported turnover more than larger ones. Since large companies pay the dominant part of value-added tax, the effects on the payment of this tax were mitigated. We find significant spillover effects to suppliers in both sectors, which are slightly stronger among larger companies.

### Summary (EN)

The Hungarian government introduced mandatory online cash registers in selected sectors in 2014, primarily in retail and accommodation and food services. Using firm level panel data, this study examined the impact of the measure on reported turnover and tax payments. Our results show that online cash registers significantly increased reported turnover, especially among smaller firms. The strongest effects were observed among the smallest companies in both analyzed sectors. However, the overall impact on VAT revenues remained moderate because large firms account for most VAT payments. Taken together, the introduction of online cash registers increased annual VAT revenues by 0.2% across the supply chain and also generated significant spillover effects.

### Summary (HU)

A magyar kormány 2014 ben egyes ágazatokban kötelezővé tette az online pénztárgépek bevezetését, elsősorban a kiskereskedelemben, valamint a szálláshely szolgáltatás és vendéglátás területén. Tanulmányunk panelökonometrai módszerekkel vizsgálta az intézkedés hatását a bevallott árbevételre és az adófizetésre vállalati szintű adatok alapján. 
Eredményeink szerint az online pénztárgépek jelentősen növelték a bevallott árbevételt, különösen a kisebb vállalkozások körében. A legerősebb hatást a legkisebb cégeknél figyeltük meg mindkét vizsgált ágazatban. Az intézkedés ugyanakkor az összes áfabevételt csak mérsékelten növelte, mivel az áfa döntő részét a nagyvállalatok fizetik be. A két ágazatot együtt vizsgálva az online pénztárgépek éves szinten 0,2 százalékkal emelték az áfabevételeket, emellett jelentős tovagyűrűző hatásokat is azonosítottunk.

The main data sources are individual VAT returns covering the period 2012–2016. In addition, we have information on the installation date of the online cash registers on the firm level from 2013 to 2015.

Ezeket összekapcsoltuk az ugyanebben az időszakban benyújtott társaságiadó bevallásokkal, valamint a 2015-ös egyedi online pénztárgép adatbázissal. Emellett vállalati szinten rendelkezésünkre álltak az online pénztárgépek telepítési időpontjára vonatkozó adatok a 2013 és 2015 közötti időszakból.

### Policy relevance (EN)

We found that the introduction of online cash registers had a remarkable effect on the reported turnover of the companies in the surveyed sectors. However, the sector- and size-specific OCR effects were heterogeneous. The introduction of online cash registers had a significant impact on reported turnover among smaller companies in the retail sector, with 10.3% growth in the 1st quintile and 2.8% in the 2nd quintile. The impact did not significantly differ from zero among the larger companies. In the AFS sector, we found the larger impact (13.6%) among the smallest group, but the effect was sizable in all other quintiles, varying from 3.2 to 7.2%. 

### Policy relevance (HU)

Eredményeink szerint az online pénztárgépek bevezetése jelentősen növelte az érintett ágazatok vállalatainak bevallott árbevételét, ugyanakkor az ágazati és vállalatméret szerinti hatások heterogének voltak. A kiskereskedelmi szektorban a hatás elsősorban a kisebb vállalkozások körében volt jelentős: az első kvintilisben 10,3 százalékos, a második kvintilisben pedig 2,8 százalékos növekedést tapasztaltunk. A nagyobb vállalatok esetében a hatás statisztikailag nem különbözött nullától. ### Abstract

Capital income represents a significant and growing share of total income at the aggregate level in most countries. However, the link between capital income and overall income inequality is not clear, as it is influenced by the distribution of capital income among individuals and its overlap with labour income. Using administrative personal income tax data, we explore the characteristics of taxable capital income in Hungary for the period from 2007 to 2021, and assess its role in overall income inequality. Capital income, which accounted for 8 to 12% of total taxable income in the period under review, was distributed among just 5 to 7% of taxpayers. The highest income percentile held 74% of capital income, while the share of the highest income decile exceeded 90% in 2021. Given its concentration at the top of the total income distribution, capital income significantly increased income inequality. By decomposing the change in inequality measured by the Gini index, we show that although the increase in overall income inequality is largely attributable to the growing concentration of labour income, capital income also exerted a major influence during this period. In our simulation, we demonstrate that an increase in the share of capital income within total income would lead to a notable increase in inequality.
### Summary (EN)

Capital income plays an important role in the structure of income in Hungary in several respects. Between 2007 and 2021, 5 to 7 per cent of taxpayers were receiving taxable capital income from investments or businesses, accounting for around one-tenth of total taxable personal income. On the other hand, capital income was much more unequally distributed among taxpayers than labour income. While the top decile received one-third of labour income, their share in capital income was 90 per cent in 2021.

A tőkejövedelem több szempontból is fontos szerepet játszik a magyarországi jövedelmi struktúrában. 2007 és 2021 között az adófizetők 5–7 százaléka rendelkezett befektetésekből vagy vállalkozási tevékenységből származó adóköteles tőkejövedelemmel, amely a teljes adóköteles személyi jövedelem mintegy egytizedét tette ki. Ugyanakkor a tőkejövedelmek eloszlása jóval egyenlőtlenebb volt az adófizetők között, mint a munkajövedelmeké. Míg a legfelső jövedelmi tized a munkajövedelmek egyharmadát kapta, addig a tőkejövedelmekből való részesedésük 2021-ben elérte a 90 százalékot.

For the years 2015 and 2021, we have a complete database of returns, while for the year 2007, we have a database containing a random sample of 10 per cent of the returns.

### Policy relevance (EN)

Our findings regarding the role of capital income in Hungary partly reflect experiences observed in other developed and transitioning countries. Capital income tends to be significantly more unevenly distributed than labour income, with concentrations primarily at the upper echelons of the income distribution. Consequently, it disproportionately contributes to overall inequality compared to its share in total income, especially when considering inequality indicators focusing on the upper strata of the income distribution

### Inequalities in regional excess mortality and life expectancy during the COVID-19 pandemic in Europe

### Abstract

Using data for 201 regions (NUTS 2) in Europe, we examine the mortality burden of the COVID-19 pandemic and how the mortality inequalities between regions changed between 2020 and 2022. We show that over the three years of the pandemic, not only did the level of excess mortality rate change considerably, but also its geographical distribution. Focusing on life expectancy as a summary measure of mortality conditions, we find that the variance of regional life expectancy increased sharply in 2021 but returned to the pre-pandemic level in 2022. The 2021 increase was due to a much higher-than-average excess mortality in regions with lower pre-pandemic life expectancy. While the life expectancy inequality has returned to its pre-pandemic level in 2022, the observed life expectancy in almost all regions is far below that expected without the pandemic.

### Summary (EN)

Did COVID-19 hit Europe's lagging regions disproportionately, and did that widen the cross-regional health gap permanently? Hajdu, Krekó and Tóth assemble data on 201 NUTS-2 regions across Europe and track regional life expectancy from 2019 through 2022. The variance of regional life expectancy spiked in 2021 — pandemic-driven excess mortality was concentrated in regions with already-low pre-pandemic life expectancy. By 2022 the variance had returned to pre-pandemic levels. The widening was sharp but not permanent. A clean documentation of how a Europe-wide health shock interacts with the existing regional health gradient.

A szélesedés éles volt, de nem tartós. Tisztán dokumentált példa arra, hogyan hat egy Európa-szintű egészségsokk a meglévő regionális egészségügyi gradiensre.

### Does sexual intimate partner violence (IPV) increase risk of multiple high-risk fertility behaviours in India: evidence from National Family Health Survey 2015–16

<!-- paper-slug: das-toth-et-al-2022-bmcph -->

- **Authors:** Milan Das, Csaba G. Tóth, Neha Shri, Mayank Singh, Babul Hossain
- **Journal:** BMC Public Health
- **Year:** 2022
- **Volume / Issue / Pages:** 22 / 1 / 2081
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12889-022-14289-0
- **URL (published):** https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-022-14289-0
- **PDF URL:** —
- **Replication URL:** —
- **Working paper series:** —
- **Topics:** demographics-migration, health, inequality-welfare
- **Methods:** descriptive-survey
- **Countries studied:** IN
- **Data types:** survey
- **Policy instruments:** —

### Abstract

One in three women from lower and middle-income countries are subjected to physical and/or sexual intimate partner violence (IPV) in their life span. Prior studies have highlighted a range of adverse health impacts of sexual IPV. However, less is known about the link between multiple high-risk fertility behaviours and sexual intimate partner violence. The present study examines the statistical association between multiple high-risk fertility behaviours and sexual intimate partner violence among women in India. The present study used a nationally representative dataset, the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-4) 2015–16. A total of 23,597 women were included in the study; a subsample of married women of reproductive age who have had at least one child 5 years prior to the survey and who had valid information about sexual IPV. Logistic regression models were employed alongside descriptive statistics. Approximately 7% of women who are or had been married face sexual IPV. The prevalence of sexual violence was higher among women who had short birth intervals and women who had given birth more than three times (12%). Around 11% of women who had experienced any high-risk fertility behaviours also experienced sexual violence. The unadjusted association suggested that multiple high-risk fertility behaviours were 32% (UORs = 1.32, 95% CI: 1.16–1.50) higher for those women who experienced sexual violence. After adjusting for other sociodemographic variables, except for women’s education and wealth quantile, the odds of multiple high-risk fertility behaviours were 16% (AOR = 1.16; 95% CI: 1.02–1.34) higher among women who faced sexual violence. The inclusion of women’s educational attainment and wealth status in the model made the association between sexual IPV and high-risk fertility behaviours insignificant. Sexual intimate partner violence is statistically associated with high-risk fertility behaviours among women in India. Programs and strategies designed to improve women’s reproductive health should investigate the different dimensions of sexual IPV in India.

### Abstract

The working mechanism of national fiscal rules depends strongly on whether a government must comply with its own rules or inherited ones. In the former case, a government usually introduces fiscal rules to show its commitment to a disciplined fiscal policy (the signaling function). In the latter context, however, inherited rules constitute external obstacles to budgetary policymaking (the limiting function). This study mainly is concerned with the limiting function and therefore bases its empirical analysis on periods when the ruling government inherited fiscal rules introduced by a previous government. The results of a panel-data econometric study indicate that national fiscal rules do contribute to disciplined fiscal policy after a change in government in times of an economic upturn. That finding, however, does not mean that the signaling function is ineffective: quite the contrary. My results, in line with the literature, indicate that the double functions of rules may complement one another. A government that introduces such rules is often already committed to a disciplined policy and wishes to signal such commitment in the short term. With the appearance of new government, however, the function of rules changes, and they efficiently promote disciplined fiscal policy in the long term.

### Summary (EN)

Do numerical fiscal rules — debt caps, deficit ceilings — keep their bite when a new government replaces the one that adopted them? Tóth assembles a panel of OECD countries 1985-2015 covering all major numerical fiscal-rule adoptions and uses government-change events to test whether inherited rules continue to constrain primary balances. The verdict: inherited rules do constrain, but the effect is roughly half the size of the constraint observed on the adopting government. Rules embedded in constitutions or supra-national frameworks (EU SGP) hold up better than statutory rules. A clean empirical case for constitutional anchoring if a government wants its fiscal-rule legacy to outlast it.

### Summary (HU)

A numerikus fiskális szabályok — adósságplafonok, hiánymaximumok — megőrzik-e szorításukat, amikor új kormány váltja le azt, amelyik elfogadta őket? Tóth OECD-országok panelját állítja össze 1985–2015 között, lefedve az összes nagyobb numerikus fiskális-szabály elfogadást, és kormányváltási eseményeket használ annak tesztelésére, hogy az örökölt szabályok továbbra is szorítják-e az elsődleges egyenleget. A verdikt: az örökölt szabályok szorítanak, de a hatás kb. fele az elfogadó kormányon megfigyelhetőnek. Az alkotmányokba vagy szupranacionális keretbe (EU SGP) ágyazott szabályok jobban kitartanak, mint a törvényi szabályok. Tiszta empirikus érv az alkotmányos beágyazás mellett, ha egy kormány azt akarja, hogy fiskális-szabályrendszere túlélje őt.
