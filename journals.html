Qualifying journals

An economist is admitted to the catalogue if their journal record clears two thresholds simultaneously.

(i) — the top-publication test: at least one publication in a Tier A (top general-interest) or Tier B (top field) journal on the list below. Tier A is roughly D1 — the top five plus their immediate peers (JEEA, RES, EJ, IER, REStat, JEP, ThE, AEAP&P, JIE). Tier B is the leading field journal in each of labor, trade, finance, macro, IO, public, development, econometrics, theory, management, urban, health, environment, and economic history.

(ii) — the volume test: at least three publications in international, peer-reviewed journals on this whitelist (any tier — A, B, or C), excluding B-hu (Hungarian-language domestic outlets like Közgazdasági Szemle or Acta Oeconomica). In practice, this means roughly the top 100 economics journals plus top finance and management outlets, as well as some broad science journals (Nature, PNAS, Science, Scientific Reports, PLOS ONE) that economists publish in.

Both (i) and (ii) must hold — a single lucky top-journal placement isn't a research career, and three workshop-tier publications aren't recognisable as one. Once admitted, all the author's peer-reviewed papers and recent (last 10 years) working papers are eligible for the catalogue. The whitelist is a guide, not a gate — borderline admissions remain editorial decisions.

Any such list is arbitrary. Suggestions welcome via GitHub issue or PR.
