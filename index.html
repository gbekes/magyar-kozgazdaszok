Who is in the collection — and how to add yourself

Short, plain-language summaries of peer-reviewed research by Hungarian economists, grouped by 15 policy topics — built for policymakers and analysts who need credible evidence quickly, with a named expert to follow up with.

Any such collection is necessarily incomplete — names will be missing and borderline calls go either way. Many excellent economists and other social scientists are absent because they publish primarily in adjacent (non-economics) journals.

If you should be here and aren't, please write in — self-suggestion welcome. File an issue or PR at the GitHub repo, or use the contribute page.

This page collects papers from Hungarian economists with at least three good publications in international economics journals, with at least one top publication. See the qualifying journals page for the exact rule and the whitelist.

How to use it

  1. Start from a topic or the search bar. The 15 policy topics give the broad map; search runs across titles, abstracts, summaries, and authors.
  2. On a paper page, read the "Policy relevance" section first. Short and bulleted — one screen tells you whether to go deeper.
  3. Check the data description and the summary next. See what country, what years, what method — that's how you judge how the result transfers to Hungary.
  4. Follow up if useful. Follow the DOI to the published paper; email the author for a follow-up.
  5. If a piece of research is interesting, just contact the researcher directly. Affiliation and contact info are on each author page.

For methodology, scope, and what this catalogue intentionally does not cover, see About.
